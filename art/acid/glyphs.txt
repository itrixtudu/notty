🌿 1. Ogham Unicode Mode

Ogham characters live in:

Unicode block: U+1680 – U+169C


Examples:

ᚁ ᚂ ᚃ ᚄ ᚅ ᚆ ᚇ ᚈ ᚉ ᚊ

Terminals can display them if:

The font supports them

The encoding is UTF-8

ANSI sequences don’t break the combining width

We absolutely can have:

--glyph-set ogham


And let it map iteration → Ogham character.

😄 2. Emoji → ANSI Art Translator

Emoji in terminals are not directly usable as glyph output for fractals (they’re often double-width, color glyphs, etc.)

But here’s the cool part:

We can translate emoji into compact ASCII/ANSI representations.

For example:

Emoji	ANSI Translation
🔥	@, #, or █ in fire palette
🌿	" or / or \
💀	[]
🌀	~ or o
⭐	* or +
🪐	(), Oo, ◯

We support this by allowing:

--glyph-emoji "🔥"


And internally:

Detect emoji sequence

Map it to ANSI glyph or glyph-set

Apply ANSI 256-color palette on top

And even better:

We can let users supply their own emoji → ANSI mapping.
--emoji-map "🔥=#" 
--emoji-map "🌿=/\\"
--emoji-map "💀=[]"


All stored in a tiny lookup table.
